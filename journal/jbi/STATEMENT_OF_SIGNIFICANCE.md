| **Problem or Issue** | Data minimization is required in clinical data use, and missing evidence is routine, but both are usually asserted (privacy is free; attention scores are value of information) rather than measured with pre-registered rules. |
| **What is Already Known** | Active feature acquisition and value-of-information have a long literature. Tabular foundation models can be used as frozen predictors. Privacy-preserving ML often reports a single accuracy drop. Single-split comparisons of models on clinical tables are common and unstable. |
| **What this Paper Adds** | A shared, content-addressed protocol that reports minimization as paired ΔAUROC with intervals and acquisition as reveal-validated ranking, across four tasks and three producers, including a real-record miniature. It shows opposite signs for the same governed step, and that replication revises both headlines. |
| **Who would benefit from the new knowledge in this paper** | Informaticians specifying how much of a record a model may see; methodologists evaluating tabular models under missingness; reviewers who need a claims boundary rather than a privacy slogan. |

Keywords (1–7): data minimization; value of information; tabular foundation models; MIMIC-IV; reproducibility; evidence acquisition
